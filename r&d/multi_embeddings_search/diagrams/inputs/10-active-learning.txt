Context:
This diagram illustrates the Active Learning loop for Rare Class Discovery for a research paper on multi-query embedding search methods for production visual retrieval. The diagram should be clear, academic, and publication-quality. Active learning is a human-in-the-loop iterative workflow that minimizes the number of human labeling decisions needed to build an effective classifier for a rare visual class in a large image database. The key insight is that by strategically selecting which candidates to show to the human annotator (uncertainty sampling), the system converges to high precision with far fewer labels than random sampling would require.

Elements to represent:
1. Round 0 — Seed gathering — The initial step at the top of the iterative loop. The human expert provides 3 to 5 seed images representing the target rare class (e.g., a specific handbag model in a specific leather). These seed images are encoded into SigLIP2 embeddings. Optionally, a text description is also encoded and combined with the image embeddings to form an initial multi-modal centroid. This seed set defines the starting point for the search. Show: a human icon, a small set of seed images, and their embedding vectors.
2. Round 1 — Initial search and labeling — The first retrieval pass. The seed embeddings are used with Rocchio or Qdrant best_score to query the database and retrieve the top 200 candidates. These candidates are presented to the human annotator in a grid or list interface. The human labels approximately 20 candidates as positive (relevant) or negative (irrelevant). This initial labeling round provides the first training data for the classifier. Show: search query, retrieved candidate grid, human labeling positive and negative.
3. Round 2 — SVM training and uncertainty sampling — The first classifier-driven round. A linear SVM is trained on all labeled examples so far (seeds plus Round 1 labels). The SVM scores every item in the database. Instead of presenting the highest-scoring candidates (which would be exploitation), the system presents candidates near the SVM decision boundary (uncertainty sampling). These are the items where the classifier is least confident, and labeling them provides maximum information gain. Show: SVM hyperplane, points near the boundary highlighted, human labeling the uncertain candidates.
4. Round 3 and beyond — Iterative refinement — Subsequent rounds follow the same pattern: retrain the SVM with all accumulated labels, score the database, present the most uncertain candidates, collect human labels. Each round shifts the decision boundary based on new information, progressively carving out the correct region in embedding space. The candidates presented become increasingly difficult edge cases, which is exactly where human judgment is most valuable. Show: updated SVM boundary, narrowing uncertainty region, continued labeling.
5. Convergence criteria — The loop terminates when one of several conditions is met: the precision on a held-out validation set exceeds a threshold (e.g., 90 percent), the number of uncertain candidates falls below a minimum, or a fixed budget of labeling rounds is exhausted. In 768-dimensional SigLIP2 embedding space, typical convergence requires 20 to 50 total labels. A practical workflow of 3 to 5 rounds of 10 to 20 labels each yields 50 to 100 total human judgments and achieves greater than 90 percent precision.
6. Human annotator — The human expert shown as a consistent element across all rounds, emphasizing their role as the oracle providing ground-truth labels. The human's time is the most expensive resource in the system, which is why active learning optimizes for minimal labeling. Show the human with a simple interface: see an image, click positive or negative.
7. Database — The large image database (potentially millions of items) shown as a background element. Only a tiny fraction of the database is ever labeled by the human. The SVM's job is to generalize from the labeled subset to score the entire database. Show: a large collection with a small highlighted subset that has been labeled.

Hierarchy:
- Principal: The iterative loop structure showing the cycle of search/score, human labeling, and classifier retraining. The loop should be the dominant visual element, making it clear that this is an iterative process with human-in-the-loop.
- Secondary: The transition from exploration (Round 1 retrieval-based) to exploitation (Round 2 and beyond uncertainty-based). The uncertainty sampling concept with candidates near the decision boundary. The convergence indicators.
- Optional: Concrete numbers (3 to 5 seeds, 20 labels per round, 3 to 5 rounds, greater than 90 percent precision). Comparison with random sampling requiring 10 times more labels. Note about using Rocchio for Round 1 and SVM for subsequent rounds.

Flow:
The diagram is organized as a circular or spiral loop flowing clockwise. At the top, Round 0 (seed gathering) feeds into Round 1 (initial search and labeling) on the right. Round 1 feeds into Round 2 (SVM training and uncertainty sampling) at the bottom. Round 2 feeds back into Round 3 (retrain and repeat) on the left, which loops back to the top for further rounds. The center of the loop contains the SVM classifier that improves with each iteration. Arrows along the loop show the data flow: seeds and labels flow in, refined classifier and better candidates flow out. A convergence indicator (e.g., precision metric rising from 60 percent to 75 percent to 90 percent) is shown along the loop progression. An exit arrow breaks out of the loop when convergence criteria are met, pointing to the final output: a trained SVM classifier and a ranked list of all database items.

Annotations:
- Round 0: 3 to 5 seed images plus optional text description, encoded into SigLIP2 embeddings
- Round 1: Rocchio or best_score retrieval of top 200 candidates, human labels approximately 20
- Round 2: Linear SVM trained on all labels, uncertainty sampling presents candidates near decision boundary (where the classifier is least confident)
- Rounds 3 to 5: iterative SVM retraining with accumulated labels, continued uncertainty sampling
- Convergence: typically 20 to 50 total labels (3 to 5 rounds of 10 to 20 labels each) achieve greater than 90 percent precision in 768-dimensional embedding space
- Uncertainty sampling: selecting candidates where the SVM's decision function value is closest to zero (i.e., nearest the hyperplane) maximizes information gain per human label
- Alternative to uncertainty sampling: diversity sampling (select candidates that are diverse from each other and from already-labeled items) can be combined with uncertainty for even faster convergence
- The SVM generalizes from the labeled subset to score the entire database, enabling rare class discovery without exhaustive manual review
