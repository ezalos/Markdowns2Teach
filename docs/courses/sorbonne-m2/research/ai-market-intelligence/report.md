Morgan Stanley's $5T by 2050 projection requires 1 billion units sold, which assumes humanoid robots become as ubiquitous as smartphones — a speculative assumption. Current production volumes are in the low thousands, making extrapolation to millions highly uncertain. The technology still faces fundamental challenges: battery life, dexterous manipulation, real-world generalization of AI models, and safety certification. Tesla's production targets (1M annually by 2027) have no precedent and Elon Musk's timeline predictions are historically optimistic. The $39B valuation of Figure AI (with minimal revenue) is a classic bubble signal. Consumer adoption timelines (Morgan Stanley: 10% of U.S. households by 2050) assume social acceptance and regulatory frameworks that do not yet exist. Chinese manufacturers' aggressive pricing ($10K-$15K) may not clear safety and quality thresholds in Western markets. Goldman Sachs itself cautions that AI progress was the primary driver of the forecast revision — if AI development plateaus, these projections become unreliable. Market research firms have commercial incentives to produce large forecast numbers.
