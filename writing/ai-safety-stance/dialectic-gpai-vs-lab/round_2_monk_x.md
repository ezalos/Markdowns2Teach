**Position:** Round 1 got the conclusion directionally right (sign GPAI) for the wrong reason. The pathway-instance frame is still second-tier thinking — still asking "which technical lane?" when Louis's revealed comparative advantage is the **three-legged policy-influence compound**: TEACHING + WRITING + INSTITUTION-BUILDING. ---

## (a) Ontological claim — what IS a career that creates durable AI-policy influence

A career that produces durable policy influence in a regulatory domain is not a position, not a pathway between positions, and not a lane. It is a **compound artifact**: a self-reinforcing triad where (1) a technical credential generates epistemic authority, (2) a canonical written output fixes a frame that decision-makers use, and (3) an institutional vehicle operationalizes the frame into enforceable practice.

Two reference cases isolate the mechanism:

- **Cathy O'Neil.** Mathematician (technical leg) → *Weapons of Math Destruction* (writing leg, the definitional book) → ORCAA (institutional leg, the auditing firm). The book gave regulators the term of art they needed; ORCAA gave them the enforcement mechanism. *Neither existed without the other.* Teaching was abandoned before the compound engaged — it wasn't load-bearing, and she knew it.
- **Simon Johnson.** MIT Sloan + former IMF chief economist (technical+institutional) → *13 Bankers* and Baseline Scenario blog (writing leg) → repeated Senate Banking Committee testimony + Dodd-Frank framing. The "cognitive capture" and "cultural capture" concepts he coined with Kwak became the language the Volcker Rule was argued through. The compound is why Johnson is cited in Dodd-Frank hearings *and* still cited in 2026 AI-concentration debates.

Neither case is "teaching of decision-makers." In both, teaching is credibility collateral — the thing that made the author trustworthy when the book dropped. But the *mechanism* of durable influence is the **artifact the compound produces**, which regulators can cite, courts can enforce against, and successor policymakers can inherit. The artifact is what survives the author's political cycle (Villani's collapse when he lost his seat is the contrast case — he had no artifact that outlived him).

**This is ontology, not rhetoric.** Regulatory-domain influence flows through *transferable artifacts*. A classroom is not transferable. A book is. An auditing standard is. An open-source verification protocol is.

The synthesis's hidden premise is that Louis is competing *at the engineering layer* — either as tier-2 frontier IC or as tier-2 governance-integrator. The pathway framing just reshuffles the question ("which tier" → "connection between tiers") without challenging the *layer*.

- **42 Network curriculum (25,000+ students, 50+ campuses)** — commissioned by Sophie Viger, who is herself a policy figure in French digital skills. This is not teaching; it is *curricular infrastructure* at national scale.
- **Former 42AI president** — institutional position, not engineering output.

What revealed preference shows is someone who keeps building **credibility-and-audience infrastructure**, then returning to engineering to anchor it. That is not the profile of a frontier IC or a governance integrator. It is the **early shape of an O'Neil/Johnson-style compound operator** — specifically the shape where teaching + institutional history are already locked in, and the question is which *artifact* anchors them.

The synthesis correctly routes Louis to GPAI. But it routes him there as a "pathway instance" — which is a framing that makes GPAI instrumentally valuable for connection purposes. That framing *tells Louis the wrong thing about what to do once inside*. It tells him to keep Kyutai warm and execute a ZKP demo cleanly and exit with a portable benchmark paper. That is second-tier advice. It produces a credential, not a compound.

**First-tier advice is: GPAI is the institution-building leg.** The ZKP demo is not a portable benchmark; it is the **technical artifact around which Louis's writing leg crystallizes over 2026–2028.** The synthesis's exit-clean-at-month-6 option reads as prudent but is actually *compound-destroying* — it treats the artifact as a credential line rather than as the nucleus of a book, a blog, a series of *Le Monde* op-eds, a MIT Technology Review piece, a FAccT paper, a *Les Echos* tribune chain like Pierre's.

## (d) Principle — compound lanes beat single-lane specialization in regulatory/policy domains

In unregulated technical domains, single-lane specialization wins. You are measured against other specialists on technical metrics; the market clears on skill. Frontier ML is such a domain for now.

In **regulatory domains**, single-lane specialization loses to compounds. The regulatory domain is not a market-of-specialists; it is a **market-of-frames**. Policy makers need the frame *more than they need the specialist* — because they must legislate, enforce, and litigate without technical depth. Whoever supplies the operative frame (the term, the definition, the standard, the audit methodology) captures the long-tail enforcement rent. O'Neil's "weapons of math destruction" is now a phrase in EU impact assessments. Johnson's "cognitive capture" is now a Senate Banking term of art. These are ten- and fifteen-year rents, paid in durable influence.

The AI governance domain in 2026 is *exactly* this kind of market. The AI Act enforcement infrastructure is being built now. Whoever supplies the operative verification frame — the term, the reference implementation, the audit methodology — captures the enforcement rent from 2026 to 2040.

Louis is not positioned to win the frontier-ML specialist market. He *is* positioned to be one of the people who supplies the operative verification frame, *if he treats GPAI as the institution-building leg of a compound rather than as a pathway transit*.

## (e) Push to extreme — Louis should spend GPAI deliberately writing toward the compound

Stop treating writing as decorative. The Round 1 synthesis mentions co-authored paper trajectory as a renewal criterion. That is insufficient. A co-authored paper is a credential; it does not anchor a compound. What anchors a compound is **a frame Louis owns** that regulators cite.

Concrete extreme-form actions during the GPAI engagement:

1. **Start a public-facing writing track from month 1.** A Substack or blog in Louis's name (not GPAI's). Cadence: one long-form piece per month. Topics: "Why ZKP verification is the AI Act's missing enforcement primitive." "What auditors will actually need to check post-2027." "The compute-governance stack Europe is quietly building." The writing is not about the work; the writing is *the frame the work will be cited under.*
2. **Target one canonical *Le Monde* or *Les Echos* tribune by month 9.** Pierre has the template. Louis should have his own byline, his own framing, his own coinage. One good term of art — a phrase that enforcement lawyers end up using — is worth more than the ZKP demo itself.
3. **Draft a book proposal by month 9.** Not a book — a *proposal*, with Trade publisher (Seuil, La Découverte, MIT Press) circulation plan. Working title irrelevant; what matters is that the proposal exists and the technical artifact anchors it. O'Neil wrote *Weapons* after ORCAA was operating. Louis writes the proposal while the demo is running, so the demo and the book co-reinforce.
4. **Convert the Sorbonne M2 into a recurring public artifact.** The slides Louis is already building are a latent compound leg. Publish them under his name with narration — not "Sorbonne course" but "Louis Develle on AI for European decision-makers." Same content, different frame. Teaching becomes broadcasting.
5. **Treat AVERI outreach not as collaboration insurance but as frame coordination.** Brundage's AVERI has a frame; Louis's GPAI work needs a complementary one. Coordinate the *language*, not just the research.

This is what the pathway-instance frame cannot see. The pathway frame treats writing as a nice-to-have parallel track. The compound frame treats writing as the *point* — the leg Louis currently lacks, the one GPAI is specifically instrumental to.
