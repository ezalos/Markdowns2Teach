### 1.2 AAAI 2025 paper — verified
- **Title**: "Multi-Agent Security Tax: Trading Off Security and Collaboration Capabilities in Multi-Agent Systems"
- **Venue**: *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence*, Vol. 39 No. 26, **AAAI-25 Special Track on AI Alignment**, pp. 27573-27581, published 2025-04-11. [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34970). [arXiv:2502.19145](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19145v2).
- **Authors**: Pierre Peigné (PRISM Eval, corresponding), Mikolaj Kniejski (Apart), Filip Sondej (Jagiellonian), Matthieu David (Apart), Jason Hoelscher-Obermaier (Apart), Christian Schroeder de Witt (**Oxford**), Esben Kran (Apart).
- **Author contribution**: Peigné led conceptualization, funding acquisition, formal analysis, visualization, project admin, and original draft. This is his paper, not a token authorship.
- **Funding**: OpenAI Research into Agentic AI Systems grant program.
- **Findings**: "Infectious malicious prompts" — compromising one LLM agent propagates system-wide (Stuxnet analogy). They test two "vaccination" defenses (false memories of safely handling malicious inputs) and two safety-instruction defenses. Conclusion: all defenses trade off capability for safety ("security tax"). Credible empirical work on a real problem, accepted at the flagship AAAI alignment track.

### 2.3 Relationship to OECD's GPAI — **NOT the same**
This is the most important disambiguation.
- **OECD GPAI** (Global Partnership on AI, gpai.ai) is the intergovernmental body founded 2020 by 15 G7+ countries, merged with OECD AI Policy Observatory in November 2024. Has Centres of Expertise in Montreal and Paris. 44 member countries. [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/about/about-gpai).
- **GPAI Policy Lab** (gpaipolicylab.org) is an independent Paris non-profit that uses "GPAI" as a technical term for "General-Purpose AI" models. No official tie to OECD GPAI. The name shares an acronym but refers to the technology category, not the intergovernmental initiative.

This is genuinely confusing. The name was likely chosen to signal domain (general-purpose AI / frontier models), but there's overlap risk. No evidence of a trademark dispute or official partnership with OECD.

## 5. Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy)

- **Rebrand**: January 2025, Open Philanthropy renamed to Coefficient Giving. [Their announcement](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/ai-program-update-navigating-transformative-ai/).
- **Scale**: $4B+ directed in grants since 2014. **The largest funder in AI existential-risk reduction** per [aisafety.com/funding](https://www.aisafety.com/funding).
- **Primary AI program**: "Navigating Transformative AI" (formerly "Potential Risks from Advanced AI"). Program leads include Max Nadeau (Technical AI Safety Associate PO).
- **Career Development and Transition Funding (CDTF)**: This is Louis's grant program. Supports 3-6 month career pivots. Named recipients include Alexandra Souly (now UK AISI Red Team), Bryce Cai (SecureBio), Robi Rahman (chem eng → AI safety), Jack Parker (math teacher → AI safety), Cecil Abungu (African AI governance). Source: [CDTF page](https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/global-catastrophic-risks-opportunities/career-development-and-transition-funding/).
- **Selectivity**: They fund extensively — MATS, BlueDot Impact, FAR.AI events, 80,000 Hours, Constellation, Apollo Research seed, university groups. Grant sizes $50K-$5M for technical AI safety RFP; career transition grants smaller (typically 3-6 months of stipend). They publicly say they want **more funders** in the space — so not artificially selective, but due-diligence driven.
- **Track record funding career transitions**: Strong. CDTF grantees consistently placed into UK AISI, SecureBio, research orgs. The capacity-building team (Abigail Bergal and others) actively writes "we'd fund more if we had more applicants".

**Green**: Funding you is a real credibility signal — they've vetted the host. Coefficient Giving also funds FAR.AI ($30M+). Their ecosystem endorses Pierre's neighborhood.
**Yellow**: They fund broadly (per Inside Philanthropy: "encourage people to err on side of applying"). Being funded by them is not exclusive per se — more like baseline vetting of the person + host combination.
