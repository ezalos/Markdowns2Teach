## a. Ontological claim — what an ML engineering career in 2026 actually is

The frontier-IC framing treats an ML engineering career as a trajectory: junior contributor → senior engineer → staff at highest-compute lab, governance as a moral tax taken later once "credentials" are built. Category error. That describes a 2018 career in a field where nobody knew yet what the technology would do to the world. That career no longer exists in 2026.

EU AI Act Article 53 enforcement goes live 2 August 2026, with fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover. An ML engineering career in 2026 is therefore **the question of which side of that infrastructure you build**. Shipping capability into an ungovernable system is one legitimate answer. Building the verification layer that makes the system governable is the other. Both are engineering. Neither is a detour from the other. Frontier-IC is not the canonical path with governance as a side-quest — it is one of two co-equal paths, and the infrastructure path is structurally younger, more neglected, and about to close.

**First: neglected-cause arbitrage dominates marginal-credential maximization over decades.** The highest-leverage move in any field is where serious work is structurally underdone, not where it is crowded. Louis can go be the 30th best post-training engineer at a European lab or the 150th at an SV startup, shipping the N+1 capability increment into a market with ~10,000 competitors. The expected-value calculation is not close. Crowded markets compete away returns; neglected infrastructure compounds them.

**Second: institutional path-dependency makes first serious voices in emerging governance spaces generationally influential.** The cryptographic reference class is explicit. Diffie and Hellman published public-key cryptography in 1976; their positioning against NSA's monopoly shaped forty years of US crypto policy. Phil Zimmermann wrote PGP in 1991, got indicted in 1993, printed the source code as a book to exploit First Amendment protection, and is why we have encrypted email. Neither was the best cryptographer of their generation on technical merits alone. They were the first serious technical voices inside an emerging governance fight, and that positioning produced decades of influence no purely-technical career at a larger institution would have produced. **Louis's GPAI moment is this reference class.** The verification infrastructure labs must comply against is being defined right now, by whoever shows up with working demonstrations. The person with a co-authored multi-GPU ZKP-of-training paper in 2026–2027 becomes the reference author every subsequent audit, every bilateral verification protocol, every compute-governance regime routes through for twenty years. That credential has a 20+ year half-life. Production ML engineer credentials have a 3–5 year half-life. Monk B's implicit bet is that Louis's near-term optics matter more than his half-life — precisely backwards for a 10-year arc.
