The scope distinction: why I focus on the control problem, not misuse
Bioweapons, propaganda, deepfakes — these are real harms, but they're fundamentally misuse problems. A human decides to do something bad with a tool. We already have frameworks for that: regulation, law enforcement, content moderation. These problems scale linearly with capability.
What I care about is categorically different: the recursive self-improvement loop from AGI to ASI, where a system capable enough to improve itself enters a feedback cycle that outpaces human ability to monitor, understand, or intervene. This isn't a misuse problem — it's a loss of control problem. It scales exponentially, and it has no precedent in human history.
The game theory trap: why unilateral restraint fails
The geopolitical landscape creates a tragic structure. Any nation that achieves AGI/ASI first gains an asymmetric advantage so large it could be civilizationally decisive. This creates three terrible incentives: every nation must participate because opting out doesn't remove the risk, it only guarantees you lose; every nation is incentivized to cut safety corners to move faster; and a single failure by whoever leads the race could be catastrophic for everyone, including the nations that weren't even competing.
This is a textbook coordination failure — identical in structure to nuclear arms races, but with higher stakes and fewer existing treaties.
My thesis: the intervention point is international coordination infrastructure
The highest-leverage contribution isn't building safer models (though that matters) or slowing down one lab (though that helps). It's giving world leaders the technical tools to coordinate. Specifically, things like zero-knowledge proof systems that allow nations to verify each other's compliance with capability thresholds without revealing proprietary research. This transforms the game theory: you move from a prisoner's dilemma (everyone defects because they can't trust others to cooperate) to a verifiable commitment game (everyone can cooperate because compliance is provable).
This work can only happen at the frontier. You need deep understanding of what capabilities look like, how training runs work, what's measurable and what's not. An outsider can't design these verification systems.
