−70.0%

SFPart 4 · One or manyThe disagreement, resolved
The disagreement, resolved

Parallelise reads. Serialise writes.

Cognition — “don't build multi-agents”

Right about writes. “Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results.” Two subagents build two mismatched halves; a third inherits the mess.[1]

Anthropic — multi-agent research

Right about reads. +90.2% over a single agent on their internal research eval — a reading task. They also state the cost: ~15× the tokens of chat.[2]

Nobody was wrong. Both stated a scoped claim as a universal.

“Read actions are inherently more parallelizable than write actions.”[3]

Bound it before it runs away

  • Three caps, all shipped defaults worth copying: 1,000 agents per run · 16 concurrent · depth of three, and no nested teams.[1][2]
  • Start at 3–5, not 15. “Three focused teammates often outperform five scattered ones.”[2]
  • Start with read-only work — reviewing, researching, investigating — before anything writes.[2]
Intuition to keep: one writer, many readers — and a cap on how many agents can exist at all.
Steal: measure your single-agent baseline before you build any orchestration.

SFPart 4 · One or manyThe five patterns
The five patterns

Five shapes, in increasing autonomy

Prompt chaining

Sequential calls, a gate between each.

Routing

Classify first, then a specialist.

Parallelization

Split the task, or vote across runs.

Orchestrator and workers

Decomposes at runtime. Central control.

Evaluator and optimizer

One generates, one grades, loop. The only one that runs unattended.

All five are doable with skills, no framework. “we recommend finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed. This might mean not building agentic systems at all.”[1]

SFPart 5 · LoopsThe shift
The shift

Stop prompting. Build loops.

From “write this for me” — one prompt, one task — to “keep working toward this”: a loop that re-prompts itself until the goal is met.

A goal

“A loop without a goal is a slop cannon.”

Context

The fuel: tools, skills, errors, memories. Consumed and replenished, not loaded once.

Evaluation

The agent verifies, not you. This is what makes it unattended.

An agent

To run it. Built into the harness now, not hand-rolled.

The evidence, both halves

Good at hill-climbing. Bad at inventing the hill.

81.7%
of the gap to a human record closed on the nanoGPT speedrun — across 153 autonomous runs and 18 frontier models, every trace published.[1]
8–9%
of human world-record progress recovered over five months — while ~77% of those records were algorithmic changes. The agents tuned hyperparameters.[2]

Time is the axis

Share of real freelance projects passed, by how long they take a human
Source: Center for AI Safety — Remote Labor Index[2]
  • Agents beat human experts 4× at a two-hour budget. Humans win at eight hours, and score 2× at thirty-two.[1]
  • But on real paid work the lines do not fall away with task length the way you would expect. Duration alone is not what makes work hard for an agent.[2]
  • Reward hacking is the failure you will actually hit. A loop optimises the metric, not the goal — the same mechanism as the incident in Part 4, at your scale.
Intuition to keep: loops buy you cheap hill-climbing at short horizons. Choosing the hill is still yours.
Steal: point a loop at a metric you already compute — never at a problem you have not defined.

SFPart 6 · The methodMaking a loop converge
Part six

The method that makes
a loop converge

Part 5 said evaluation is the load-bearing ingredient. This is how you actually get one.

SFPart 6 · The methodThe spine
The spine

Clarify, draft, then evaluate, observe, improve

1 · Clarify

Deep-research what separates a great output from an average one. Take the 20% that carries 80%. Turn it into binary checks that sum to a score.

2 · Draft

The smallest thing that runs A to Z. Wire tools and data now; build the checks in from the first run.

3 · Evaluate

Three fresh examples, clean context. Otherwise the conversation does the work and it breaks “mysteriously” tomorrow.

4 · Observe

Criteria down, examples across. A red row is a systematic gap; a red column is a broken example.

5 · Improve

Exactly one bottleneck, the most pressing, then stop. A change that does not move the tests does not stay.

6 · Understand

Have it teach you and quiz you. A system you cannot explain is one you cannot fix or defend.

Intuition to keep: the agent did the typing — make sure you still own the thinking.
Steal: three test cases, in a folder, replayed after every change, forever.
Sources : Hamel Husain — your AI product needs evals · Braintrust — the maturity phases of running evals

SFPart 7 · LeverageAnd it holds up measured
And it holds up when measured

The threshold, not the winner

  • An agent that writes the conversation to a file and greps it scores 74.0% on the standard memory benchmark — in the same range as the specialised products, and above the full-context baseline.[1]
  • The honest threshold: at ~26,000 tokens plain full context wins outright; at ~115,000 memory systems win by 11–24 points. Scale when you cross it, not before.[2]
  • Treat the vendor tables carefully — an audit of that benchmark found 6.4% of the answer key wrong and the judge accepting 62.81% of deliberately wrong answers.[3]
The category is also not six months old, whatever the marketing says. Its founding paper is from October 2023.[4]

SFPart 7 · LeverageWrap
Part 7 · wrap

Two more assets, then the meta-skill

Skills that improve themselves

A 9B model with a persistent wiki and executable skills scores 47.4%; a 27B model without them scores 39.4%. Three times the parameters, and it still loses — the written artefact carries the gain.[1]

The distribution nobody optimises

AI assistants are still under 0.15% of visits, but those visitors convert 60% higher, and the channel grew 66% in a year. Tiny, best-converting, fastest-growing — the profile of a channel worth a scheduled agent, not a headcount.[2][3]

Intuition to keep: the edge is not the model — everyone has that one. It is noticing, first, what it just made possible.
Steal: pick one thing that was intractable twelve months ago and re-test it this week.
