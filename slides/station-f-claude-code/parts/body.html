The disagreement, resolved

Parallelise reads. Serialise writes.

Cognition — “don't build multi-agents”

Right about writes. “Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results.” Two subagents build two mismatched halves; a third inherits the mess.[1]

Anthropic — multi-agent research

Right about reads. +90.2% over a single agent on their internal research eval — a reading task. They also state the cost: ~15× the tokens of chat.[2]

Nobody was wrong. Both stated a scoped claim as a universal.

SFLoopsWhy now
Why now

Stop prompting. Build loops.

From “write this for me” — one prompt, one task — to “keep working toward this”: a loop that re-prompts itself until the goal is met. Three things changed this year, and all three are measurable.

The models can hold a long task

On an 8-day autonomous run, the best model closed 81.7% of the gap to a human record — 811 experiments, unattended.[1] Two years ago a run like that collapsed in an afternoon.

The loop is now shipped, not hand-rolled

/loop, routines and scheduled runs are product features. You no longer write the driver, the retry logic or the state file yourself.[2]

And the cost of being wrong collapsed

Same capability, 9× to 900× cheaper per year.[3] A loop that burns tokens overnight to find one real improvement is now an obviously good trade.

The ingredient people skip is the one that makes it work unattended: the agent has to be able to check itself. A loop without a verifier is just a more expensive way to be wrong.
Sources : [1] Prime Intellect · [2] Claude Code — routines · [3] Epoch AI — inference price trends

SFLoopsWhat actually separates them
What actually separates them

Everyone finds the same ideas. The experiment discipline decides.

Share of the human record gap closed, by agent time
153 runs, 18 models, up to 9 days each. Source: Prime Intellect[1]

“The models all find similar ideas. What separates them is how they run experiments.”[1]

1 · They measure their own noise

62 of ~100 runs re-measured the benchmark's noise instead of trusting the number given to them — and those runs cluster at the top of the table.[1]

2 · They don't kill an idea on one seed

Weak runs bin a whole family after one bad seed, or treat their own crash as proof. Strong ones re-test borderline results on three seeds, and pay for eight only when the noise model says it is worth it.[1]

3 · They go back and re-test

After every merge they re-ablate and drop what stopped helping, and they revisit old negatives when the recipe changes. One late re-probe by Fable 5 was worth 31 steps.[1]

The honest ceiling: not one of the 153 runs invented a genuinely new method — every winning ingredient already existed in the literature.[1] Loops climb a hill you can already measure. Choosing the hill is still yours.
Sources : [1] Prime Intellect — measuring autonomous research

SFCloseThe whole arc
What to take away

From a loop, to a harness, to loops

  • An agent is a loop: gather context → act → verify, until the goal is met. The model is a commodity — the harness is the engineering.
  • Stop hand-writing steps. Build loops that prompt themselves — and a loop is only as good as its verifier.
  • Two ways to work: skills-piped feature work, and autonomous experiment loops — both start from a sharp definition of done.

The bottleneck was never code. It’s knowing what “done” means.
